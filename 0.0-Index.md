## 1. Project name

**iRead**

An EPUB reading app for iPad that lets readers skim descriptive passages and still follow the story: phrases they may not follow are underlined, and a tap shows the author's own earlier sentence that explains them.

---

## 2. Background

### Who it is for

The primary users are **fiction readers with aphantasia**, people who cannot form mental images. Two groups matter:

- **Hobby readers** who already read fiction regularly.
- **New readers** who want to start reading fiction but find it hard to follow.

### Research

The problem below comes from **desk research** on the international aphantasia forum [aphantasia.com](https://aphantasia.com/). [TBD: research period, and how many threads or posts were reviewed]

- **Assumption dropped.** We first assumed iRead should create **visual imagery** so readers with aphantasia could enjoy fiction. The research showed otherwise: they have their own ways of enjoying a novel and do not need pictures. This is why iRead does not generate images or illustrations.
- **Skimming.** Description passages about characters or scenery are likely to be skipped.
- **Searching back.** When a skipped description is mentioned again at an important moment, they look for the answer in earlier paragraphs, one by one.

These findings led to two insights: readers need to **recognize which character** is meant (PRD 3.1 Symbols), and they struggle to **find the paragraph** that answers their question (PRD 2.1 Reference Passage).

### The problem

Readers with aphantasia tend to **skim long descriptive passages**: scenery, character appearance, and world building. How much they skim depends on how the author tells the story.

The trouble comes later. Authors often refer back to a person or thing by appearance instead of by name, for example *"the red short hair girl"* or *"the rusty door"*. A reader who skimmed the original description no longer knows who or what is meant.

### What readers do today

When a phrase confuses them, readers scroll or flip back and re-read earlier pages, one by one, until they find the sentence that first introduced that person or thing. This is:

- **Time-consuming**, because there is no shortcut to the right spot.
- **Disruptive**, because it breaks the flow of reading.
- **Risky with search**, because in-book search needs the exact words and also shows later matches that can spoil the story.

### What iRead does

While the reader reads, iRead **underlines phrases a skimming reader may not follow**: an action, a reaction, a feeling, an outcome, a place or thing, or a person. A phrase is underlined only if iRead can point to the author's own earlier sentence that explains it. The reader **taps the underline**, and on the same page a card shows that sentence. The reader can jump to it and return.

iRead **does not generate an answer**. It points the reader to the sentence the author wrote. It only uses text **before the underlined sentence**, so it cannot spoil what comes next (PRD 2.1 §5.4).

### Why Apple

The goal is to build fully on the Apple ecosystem and use what the reader's own device can do: on-device language processing (Natural Language framework and Apple's on-device foundation model), so book content stays on the device.

### Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Underline** | A phrase on the page that a skimming reader may not follow, and that has an earlier sentence explaining it. It is usually an event, not only a character. See PRD 2.1 §1. |
| **Earlier passage** | The author's sentence shown on the card, also called the reference. It always comes **before** the underlined sentence, in the same chapter. |
| **Explains** | A skimming reader, shown the passage after the phrase, would say "ah, that's why" or "ah, that's what that is". A sentence that is only on the same topic, or uses similar words, does not count. |
| **Descriptive reference** | A phrase that refers to a person or thing by appearance or description instead of by name, e.g. "the red short hair girl". |
| **Read boundary** | For an underline in sentence `s`, only sentences before `s` may be used. |
| **Reading frontier** | The furthest sentence the reader has seen (PRD 1.3). Still saved, but no feature reads it right now. |

**Example (Little Women, Chapter 1)**

- Earlier: *"…thinking of Father far away, where the fighting was."*
- Later: *"…said Jo, thinking that keeping her temper at home was a much harder task than facing a rebel or two down South."*

"facing a rebel or two down South" is the kind of phrase iRead aims to underline, with the earlier sentence on its card.

## 3. Success metrics

| # | Metric | Target | How it is measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Accuracy** | **Mismatch rate ≤ 5%**: of the underlines shown, how many point to a sentence that doesn't explain the phrase. **[Proposed]** **Not met today:** 7 of the 10 references shown were wrong. | Golden set of 67 phrases on Little Women chapter 1, with labels still to be reviewed by the product owner (PRD 2.1 §4.3). |
| 2 | **No spoilers** | **0** sentences at or after the underlined sentence are scored, sent to the model, or shown. This is a hard requirement, not a target. | Automated check in the harness, on the logged candidates and prompts (PRD 2.1 §4.3, §5.4). |
| 3 | **Readers keep reading** | Readers with aphantasia complete **more chapters** with iRead than without it. | **Pilot phase, after the demo.** The app counts chapters finished **on the device**; testers choose to share their stats through an in-app export, plus a short survey. The feature can be switched off per tester to compare with and without (e.g. 1 week each). **[Proposed]** Export and switch are not built. |

Speed compared to flipping back manually is part of the problem statement but is not a headline metric.

### How iRead is different

Kindle and Google Play Books use AI to **answer the question with text the AI writes**. iRead **does not write an answer**; it **takes the reader to the sentence the author wrote** that explains the phrase, then lets them return.

What follows from that **[Proposed]**:

- **No made-up facts.** The result is always the author's own sentence.
- **One tap, one result.** No question to type. The reader doesn't have to notice what they missed, because the phrase is already underlined.
- **Any EPUB the reader imports**, not tied to one store, country, or title list.
- **On-device.** Book content is processed on the iPad, not sent to a server.
- **Built for aphantasic readers** from the start.

## 6. Constraints and decisions

| Decision | Detail |
|---|---|
| Platform | iPadOS native, Swift, SwiftUI, MVVM. **iPad only for this development.** iPhone (iOS) and macOS are **TBD** and need their own decision before any work starts. |
| Reference engine | **UnderlineEngine is the app's only reference engine** on the trial branch. It replaces the character engine (characters, mentions, symbols). Whether to merge it is not decided (§7). |
| First release | **Demoable app** on iPad, matching the "Demoable app" release target of PRD 1.0 to 2.1. The community pilot through TestFlight comes **after the demo**. **[Proposed]** No onboarding polish, monetisation, or App Store review needed yet. |
| Pilot measurement (after the demo) | Not part of the demo scope: the chapter-finished event and the per-tester on/off switch for Reference Passage are added when the pilot is planned. No analytics server. Reading stats stay on the device until the tester chooses to export and share them. Reference Passage has an on/off switch for the with/without comparison. **[Proposed]** |
| Minimum OS | iPadOS 26. **[Proposed]** |
| Device requirement | Underlines work on any supported iPad. With Apple Intelligence (Foundation Models), a model picks and confirms each reference. Without it, rules do both, with fewer and more cautious underlines (PRD 2.1 A4). Import, Library, and Reader work on any supported device. **[Proposed]** |
| On-device processing | Book content is processed on the device using Apple's frameworks. "Built on the Apple ecosystem" covers AI processing and data; it does not rule out third-party libraries. |
| EPUB parsing and rendering | **Built in-house** (decided): `EPUBKit`, a package in the app repo on top of ZIPFoundation (PRD 1.1), and `PagerSvc`, a paginator on TextKit (PRD 1.3). Epic 2 needs sentence-level positions, tap targets, and underlines on the exact text, so the app keeps full control instead of mapping positions through a third-party toolkit. A toolkit such as Readium can be reconsidered later. |
| Book language | **English** books only for the demo and the pilot. Test novels for the accuracy metric are English. The app doesn't detect a book's language yet (PRD 1.1). |
| Supported files | DRM-free EPUB files only. Store-bought EPUBs are usually copy-protected, and Kindle books are not EPUB. The app bundles 5 public-domain sample books. It doesn't check for DRM yet (PRD 1.1). **[Proposed]** |
| No spoilers | The limit is the **read boundary**: only sentences before the underlined sentence are searched, scored, or shown (PRD 2.1 §5.4). Analysis runs ahead of the reader, but each underline only uses earlier text. The card shows nothing else about the story. |

### Known technical risks

- **In-house pagination.** Building the paginator and EPUB parser ourselves takes more work than using a toolkit and must handle varied EPUB markup, font size changes, and iPad rotation and Split View / Stage Manager (PRD 1.1 §8, PRD 1.3 §5.4).
- **Accuracy.** 7 of the 10 references shown are wrong on the unreviewed golden set. Tuning hasn't started (PRD 2.1 §9).
- **No character identity.** UnderlineEngine finds an explaining sentence directly and doesn't know who anyone is. Apple's Natural Language framework has **no coreference resolution**, so linking "the red short hair girl" to a character would need its own engine again.
- **Mac and iPad results differ.** Chapter 1 of Little Women gives 60 underlines on a Mac and 121 on an iPad. The cause is unknown, so accuracy should be judged on iPad.
- Apple's on-device foundation model has a small context window (about **4,096 tokens**, input and output combined, on current models; it can differ per OS version, so PRD 2.1 §5.3 reads it at runtime), so a whole book cannot be passed in. Candidate sentences must be narrowed down first.
- A developer forum report says Apple's English sentence embedding returns nil on some physical iOS 27 devices; worth testing early, including on iPadOS 27. PRD 2.1 §5.6 uses embeddings for ranking only, tries word vectors next, then falls back to shared words.

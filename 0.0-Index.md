## 1. Project name

**Skimmaxing**

An EPUB reading app for iPhone that lets readers skim descriptive passages without losing track of who or what the story is talking about.

---

## 2. Background

### Who it is for

The primary users are **fiction readers with aphantasia**, people who cannot form mental images. Two groups matter:

- **Hobby readers** who already read fiction regularly.
- **New readers** who want to start reading fiction but find it hard to follow.

### The problem

Readers with aphantasia tend to **skim long descriptive passages**: scenery, character appearance, and world building. How much they skim depends on how the author tells the story.

The trouble comes later. Authors often refer back to a person or thing by appearance instead of by name, for example *"the red short hair girl"* or *"the rusty door"*. A reader who skimmed the original description no longer knows who or what is meant.

### What readers do today

When a phrase confuses them, readers scroll or flip back and re-read earlier pages, one by one, until they find the sentence that first introduced that person or thing. This is:

- **Time-consuming**, because there is no shortcut to the right spot.
- **Disruptive**, because it breaks the flow of reading.
- **Risky with search**, because in-book search needs the exact words and also shows later matches that can spoil the story.

### What Skimmaxing does

The reader **highlights a confusing phrase**. On the same page, Skimmaxing shows the **First Mention**: the author's own sentence that introduced who or what the phrase refers to. The reader can jump to that sentence and jump back.

Skimmaxing **does not generate an answer**. It points the reader to the paragraph the author wrote. It only looks at text **before** the reader's current position, so it cannot spoil what comes next.

### Why Apple

The goal is to build fully on the Apple ecosystem and use what the reader's own device can do: on-device language processing (Natural Language framework and Apple's on-device foundation model), so book content stays on the device.

### Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **First Mention** | The earlier sentence where the author **first gave the description** that the reader highlighted, which identifies the same person or thing. It is not simply the person's first appearance. Linguists call this the *antecedent*; the product uses "First Mention". See the example below. |
| **Descriptive reference** | A phrase that refers to a person or thing by appearance or description instead of by name, e.g. "the red short hair girl". |
| **Current position** | Where the reader is in the book. Nothing after this point is ever used. |

**First Mention example (definition of done)**

- Page 3: *"Mira arrived at the station."* (first appearance, no description)
- Page 5: *"Mira, a girl with short red hair, pushed through the crowd."* (description first given)
- Page 40: *"The red short hair girl smiled."* (reader highlights "red short hair girl")

Correct result: the **page 5** sentence, because that is where the author first described the girl this way. Page 3 is not the First Mention, because it does not contain the description.

## 3. Success metrics

| # | Metric | Target | How it is measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Accuracy** | At least **80%** of First Mention results point to the correct introducing sentence. **[Proposed]** | Test set of **3 sample novels**, about **20 descriptive phrases each**, with the correct First Mention marked by hand. **[Proposed]** |
| 2 | **No spoilers** | **0** results taken from text after the reader's current position. This is a hard requirement, not a target. | Automated check on the same test set: every result's location must come before the highlighted phrase. |
| 3 | **Readers keep reading** | Readers with aphantasia complete **more chapters** with Skimmaxing than without it. | Community pilot. The app counts chapters finished **on the device**; testers choose to share their stats through an in-app export, plus a short survey. The feature can be switched off per tester to compare with and without (e.g. 1 week each). **[Proposed]** |

Speed compared to flipping back manually is part of the problem statement but is not a headline metric.

### How Skimmaxing is different

Kindle and Google Play Books use AI to **answer the question with text the AI writes**. Skimmaxing **does not write an answer**; it **takes the reader to the paragraph the author wrote** that resolves the confusion, then lets them jump back.

What follows from that **[Proposed]**:

- **No made-up facts.** The result is always the author's own sentence.
- **One highlight, one result.** No question to type.
- **Any EPUB the reader imports**, not tied to one store, country, or title list.
- **On-device.** Book content is processed on the iPhone, not sent to a server.
- **Built for aphantasic readers** from the start.

## 6. Constraints and decisions

| Decision | Detail |
|---|---|
| Platform | iOS native, Swift, SwiftUI, MVVM. |
| First release | Community pilot, distributed through TestFlight. **[Proposed]** No onboarding polish, monetisation, or App Store review needed yet. |
| Pilot measurement | No analytics server. Reading stats stay on the device until the tester chooses to export and share them. Feature Reference has an on/off switch for the with/without comparison. **[Proposed]** |
| Minimum OS | iOS 26. **[Proposed]** |
| Device requirement | Feature Reference requires a device with Apple Intelligence. Import, Library, and Reader work on any supported device; Feature Reference shows that it is unavailable when Apple Intelligence is off or unsupported. **[Proposed]** |
| On-device processing | Book content is processed on the device using Apple's frameworks. "Built on the Apple ecosystem" covers AI processing and data; it does not rule out third-party libraries. |
| EPUB rendering | Use an existing EPUB toolkit (Readium Swift Toolkit or similar) instead of building a layout engine. |
| Book language | **English** books only for the pilot. Test novels for the accuracy metric are English. |
| Supported files | DRM-free EPUB files only. Store-bought EPUBs are usually copy-protected, and Kindle books are not EPUB. Where readers get DRM-free books (e.g. public-domain catalogues) is left to PRD 0.2. **[Proposed]** |
| No spoilers | The limit is the **location of the highlighted phrase**: only text before it is searched or shown. Jumping back to a First Mention or re-reading does not move the limit. **[Proposed]** |

### Known technical risks

- Apple's Natural Language framework recognises names of people, places, and organisations, but has **no coreference resolution** (linking "the red short hair girl" back to her introduction). This part must be built on top of Apple's tools.
- Apple's on-device foundation model has a context window of about **4,096 tokens** (input and output combined), so a whole book cannot be passed in. Candidate sentences must be narrowed down first.
- A developer forum report says Apple's English sentence embedding returns nil on some physical iOS 27 devices; worth testing early.
